What is football form? Why goal difference beats W-D-L

How to read a form guide, why goal difference over the last five games tells you more than wins and losses, and how to use it.

By HeyFootball team📅 18 October 2026⏱️ 2 min read
📈

"Form" is one of the most used words in football. A team "in form" is playing well right now, whatever their history or reputation. But how do you actually measure it? Here's how to read form like an analyst, and why HeyFootball's Form tab uses goal difference.

The classic form guide: W-D-L

Most sites show form as a string of letters for the last five games, newest first, for example W W D L W. It's quick to read: that team has won three of its last five.

The problem? A letter hides the details. A last-minute 1-0 win and a 5-0 demolition both show as "W".

Why goal difference is better

Goal difference (goals scored minus goals conceded) shows how well a team is playing, not just whether it won. Compare two teams over five games:

TeamResultsW-D-LGoal difference
Team A1-0, 1-0, 0-0, 0-1, 1-0W W D L W+2
Team B3-0, 4-1, 2-2, 1-2, 3-1W W D L W+7

Both have identical letters, but Team B is clearly playing much better. Goal difference spots that straight away. That's why the Form tab ranks teams by goal difference over their last five games.

How to use form

  • Predictions: a team in strong form at home is a good bet for a win. See our score prediction tips.
  • Your CL bracket: form can show which big clubs are struggling and which smaller clubs are flying. Read the Champions League guide.
  • Spotting trends: a team whose goal difference keeps improving is getting better, even if the results haven't come yet.

Form's limits

Five games is a small sample. A team may have played three easy opponents in a row, or rested players for a cup match. Use form alongside the league table, home and away records, and common sense.
